# Zomato: The Next Order Effect
### A Study of Customer Behaviour, Brand Preference and Food Delivery Experience

**An independent MBA customer research project exploring what influences a customer's next food delivery decision.**

---

## 1. About the Project

Why does a customer choose Zomato today and Swiggy the next time?

Food delivery platforms compete on more than the number of restaurants available. Discounts, final payable prices, delivery reliability and the overall app experience can all influence a customer's decision.

I wanted to understand what actually matters to customers when they decide where to order from. Instead of relying only on assumptions, I conducted a customer survey, analysed the responses and converted the findings into practical business recommendations.

This project explores the factors that influence platform preference, customer satisfaction and the possibility of repeat orders.

**The central research question:**

*What makes a customer place their next food order on Zomato rather than choose a competitor?*

## 3. Research Objectives

The study focused on five questions:

1. What makes customers prefer one food delivery platform over another?
2. How important are discounts, coupons and additional charges?
3. What problems do customers commonly face while ordering food?
4. How do customers who have used Zomato rate their experience?
5. What improvements could encourage customers to order from Zomato more often?

## 4. Key Research Findings

### Finding 1: A close contest between Zomato and Swiggy

- Zomato was selected as the most-used platform by 17 respondents (32.7%).
- Swiggy was also selected by 17 respondents (32.7%).
- Nine respondents (17.3%) used both platforms equally.

**What this suggests:** Platform preference may not be fixed. Customers can consider alternatives depending on the value and experience offered.

### Finding 2: Discounts influence platform preference

- 38 out of 52 respondents (73.1%) considered discounts very or extremely important.
- Better discounts and offers were the most frequently selected reason for preferring a platform, with 21 responses (40.4%).

**What this suggests:** Offers are an important part of the customer decision, but relying only on discounts may not be a sustainable way to encourage repeat orders.

### Finding 3: Customers pay attention to the final bill

- 20 respondents (38.5%) identified high delivery or additional charges as their biggest ordering problem.
- 29 respondents (55.8%) selected either a lower final bill or available discounts and coupons when choosing between apps offering the same restaurant.

**What this suggests:** Customers evaluate the amount they actually pay, not just the price displayed against a food item.

### Finding 4: Customers switch between platforms

- 21 respondents (40.4%) said they switch between platforms sometimes or frequently.
- 18 respondents (34.6%) said they switch rarely.
- 13 respondents (25%) said they never switch.

**What this suggests:** There is an opportunity to understand what makes customers reconsider their platform choice. The survey does not establish the direction or reason for each switch.

### Finding 5: Zomato satisfaction leaves room for further exploration

Among the 45 respondents who had used Zomato and provided a satisfaction rating:

- 24 (53.3%) were satisfied or very satisfied.
- 19 (42.2%) were neutral.
- 2 (4.4%) were dissatisfied.

**What this suggests:** The neutral customer group deserves further attention. Understanding what would improve their experience could help identify opportunities to strengthen preference.

## 5. From Insights to Business Recommendations

The findings led me to identify five areas worth testing.

| Opportunity | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Price transparency | Make the final payable amount and applicable charges easier to understand before checkout. |
| Targeted offers | Experiment with relevant offers for selected customer groups instead of depending entirely on blanket discounts. |
| Delivery confidence | Test proactive delay notifications and more realistic delivery estimates. |
| Customer support | Make issue resolution easier to track and provide human escalation for unresolved problems. |
| Reordering experience | Conduct usability testing on restaurant discovery and repeat-order journeys. |

These are proposed opportunities for experimentation, not claims that Zomato currently lacks these features.

## 6. How Success Could Be Measured

Recommendations need measurable outcomes. I identified the following KPIs for future testing:

- **Checkout completion rate:** Understand whether clearer pricing improves checkout completion.
- **Fee-related abandonment:** Track whether customers leave after seeing additional charges.
- **30-day repeat order rate:** Measure whether targeted offers contribute to incremental repeat orders.
- **Contribution margin:** Ensure that promotional activity creates business value.
- **On-time delivery rate:** Monitor delivery reliability.
- **ETA accuracy:** Compare estimated and actual delivery times.
- **Issue resolution time:** Understand how quickly customer problems are resolved.
- **Customer satisfaction:** Track changes in customer experience after improvements.

## 10. Research Limitations

I believe it is important to be transparent about what this research can and cannot establish.

- The study received 52 responses and used convenience sampling.
- Chennai was the intended research geography, but respondent location was not collected. Therefore, Chennai-only coverage cannot be verified.
- The sample is age-skewed: 53.8% of respondents were aged 18–22 and 32.7% were above 45.
- Responses are self-reported and may not reflect actual ordering behaviour.
- The study does not contain actual transaction, conversion, retention or operational performance data.
- Open-ended feedback was manually coded and should be treated as directional rather than statistically representative.

The findings are exploratory signals and should not be generalised to all Zomato customers.

## 11. What I Learned

This project made me look at customer behaviour beyond the usual idea that discounts bring customers back.

A customer may like a platform, but the next order can still depend on the final bill, the expected delivery experience and how easily a problem gets resolved.

Three things stood out to me:

1. **Preference and loyalty are different.** Customers can have a preferred platform and still consider alternatives.
2. **The final bill matters.** A discount may attract attention, but additional charges can influence the final decision.
3. **Research should lead to action.** Collecting responses is only the beginning. The real value comes from turning those responses into business questions that can be tested and measured.
